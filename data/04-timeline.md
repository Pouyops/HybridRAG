# Mission Timeline

All times below are given in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), with approximate U.S. Eastern Daylight Time (EDT) noted where useful. EDT was four hours behind UTC in July 1969.

## Launch

Apollo 11 launched from Pad 39A at Kennedy Space Center on July 16, 1969, at approximately 13:32 UTC (9:32 a.m. EDT). The Saturn V described in `03-spacecraft.md` carried the crew first into a parking orbit around Earth. Roughly two and a half hours after liftoff, the S-IVB third stage reignited for the Trans-Lunar Injection burn, sending the Command and Service Module Columbia and the attached Lunar Module Eagle out of Earth orbit toward the Moon.

## Coast to the Moon and Lunar Orbit Insertion

The three-day coast to the Moon included a transposition and docking maneuver, in which Columbia separated from the spent third stage, turned around, and docked nose-to-nose with Eagle to extract it for the rest of the journey. On July 19, 1969, Apollo 11 fired Columbia's main engine to slow down and enter lunar orbit, a maneuver called Lunar Orbit Insertion, placing the spacecraft in an orbit that brought it as close as about 110 kilometers (60 nautical miles) to the lunar surface.

## Undocking and Descent

On July 20, 1969, Armstrong and Aldrin entered Eagle, and the two spacecraft undocked from one another at approximately 17:44 UTC while Collins remained aboard Columbia. Eagle's descent engine fired to begin powered descent roughly an hour later. During the final approach, onboard computer alarms (later identified as a rendezvous-radar data overload, not a critical fault) sounded repeatedly, and Armstrong took semi-manual control to fly Eagle past a boulder-strewn crater to a clearer landing spot. Eagle touched down in the Sea of Tranquility at approximately 20:17 UTC (4:17 p.m. EDT), roughly two and a half hours after undocking from Columbia. Armstrong's radio call, "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed," confirmed the touchdown to Mission Control.

## Moonwalk

Armstrong opened Eagle's hatch and began exiting several hours after landing. He set foot on the lunar surface at approximately 02:56 UTC on July 21, 1969 (10:56 p.m. EDT on July 20), speaking the words examined in `05-landing-and-moonwalk.md`. Aldrin joined him on the surface about 19 minutes later. The two astronauts spent roughly two and a half hours outside Eagle together before returning inside.

## Ascent and Rendezvous

Eagle's ascent stage lifted off from the lunar surface on July 21, 1969, at approximately 17:54 UTC, leaving the descent stage behind as a launch platform. After a few hours of orbital maneuvering, Eagle's ascent stage redocked with Columbia at approximately 21:35 UTC. Armstrong and Aldrin transferred back into Columbia with their samples and equipment, and the ascent stage was then jettisoned into lunar orbit.

## Return and Splashdown

Columbia's main engine fired for Trans-Earth Injection on July 22, 1969, sending the crew back toward Earth. After a return coast of roughly two and a half days, the Command Module separated from the Service Module and reentered Earth's atmosphere. Apollo 11 splashed down in the Pacific Ocean on July 24, 1969, at approximately 16:50 UTC (12:50 p.m. EDT), about 1,500 kilometers southwest of Hawaii. The crew was recovered by the aircraft carrier USS Hornet, where they entered a mobile quarantine facility as a precaution against possible lunar contamination.
