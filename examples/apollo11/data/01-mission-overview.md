# Apollo 11 Mission Overview

Apollo 11 was the American spaceflight that first landed humans on the Moon. It was flown by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) and launched from Kennedy Space Center in Merritt Island, Florida, on July 16, 1969. The mission was the fifth crewed flight of the Apollo program and the third to leave Earth orbit for the Moon, following the lunar-orbit rehearsals of Apollo 8 and Apollo 10.

## Purpose of the Mission

Apollo 11's central objective was to fulfill a goal President John F. Kennedy set before the U.S. Congress in May 1961: to land a man on the Moon and return him safely to Earth before the decade was out. Beyond the political goal of the Space Race with the Soviet Union, the mission was designed to demonstrate that a crewed lunar landing and surface stay were technically achievable, to collect the first samples of lunar rock and soil, to deploy scientific instruments on the surface, and to prove out the rendezvous and docking techniques needed to get the astronauts home.

## Program Context

Apollo 11 sat within the broader Apollo program, which ran from 1961 to 1972 and used the Saturn family of rockets developed under Wernher von Braun at NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center. Earlier missions tested hardware in Earth orbit (Apollo 7) and lunar orbit (Apollo 8, Apollo 10) without landing. Apollo 11 combined all of those proven pieces for the first time: a crewed lunar orbit insertion, a powered descent to the surface, a moonwalk, a lunar liftoff, and a rendezvous back with the orbiting command ship.

## Administering Agency

NASA managed the mission through its Office of Manned Space Flight, with flight operations directed from the Mission Control Center at the Manned Spacecraft Center in Houston, Texas (renamed the Johnson Space Center in 1973). Launch operations were run from Kennedy Space Center.
