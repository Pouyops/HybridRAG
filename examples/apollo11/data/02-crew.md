# The Apollo 11 Crew

Apollo 11 carried three astronauts, all veterans of NASA's earlier Gemini program: Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin, and Michael Collins. Each held a distinct role, and only two of the three ever set foot on the Moon.

## Neil Armstrong, Commander

Neil Armstrong was the mission commander and the first human to step onto the lunar surface. Born in 1930 in Wapakoneta, Ohio, he was a former U.S. Navy fighter pilot who flew combat missions in the Korean War and later worked as a test pilot for NASA's predecessor, NACA, flying experimental high-speed aircraft such as the X-15. He first flew in space in 1966 as commander of Gemini 8, during which he executed the first docking of two spacecraft in orbit and then handled a dangerous uncontrolled spin by performing the first emergency landing of a U.S. crewed mission. On Apollo 11, Armstrong was responsible for the overall mission and personally took manual control of the Lunar Module during the final phase of descent to steer it past a boulder field to a safe landing site.

## Buzz Aldrin, Lunar Module Pilot

Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin was the Lunar Module Pilot and the second person to walk on the Moon. Born in 1930 in Montclair, New Jersey, he graduated from the U.S. Military Academy at West Point and flew Air Force fighter jets in the Korean War before earning a doctorate in astronautics from MIT, where his research focused on orbital rendezvous techniques. He put that expertise to direct use in 1966 on Gemini 12, where he performed a record-setting spacewalk that proved astronauts could work productively outside a spacecraft. On Apollo 11, Aldrin operated alongside Armstrong inside the Lunar Module Eagle during descent and joined him on the lunar surface roughly 19 minutes after Armstrong's first step.

## Michael Collins, Command Module Pilot

Michael Collins was the Command Module Pilot and did not land on the Moon. Born in 1930 in Rome, Italy, to a U.S. Army family, he was also an Air Force fighter pilot before joining NASA's astronaut corps. He had flown Gemini 10 in 1966, performing two spacewalks. On Apollo 11, Collins remained in lunar orbit aboard the Command Module Columbia while Armstrong and Aldrin descended to the surface in the Lunar Module Eagle. For roughly 21.5 hours, Collins orbited the Moon alone, passing behind its far side once per orbit and losing all radio contact with both Earth and his crewmates during those periods. His job was to keep Columbia ready for the critical rendezvous and docking that would bring the three men back together for the trip home; had Eagle been unable to lift off or dock, Collins would have had to return to Earth without them.

## Crew Selection and Backup Crew

All three men were assigned to Apollo 11 in January 1969. The backup crew consisted of Jim Lovell as commander, William Anders as Command Module Pilot, and Fred Haise as Lunar Module Pilot; Lovell would later command the Apollo 13 mission. NASA's rotation system meant the Apollo 11 prime crew had previously served as backup for Apollo 8, which followed a standard pattern of advancing crews through the flight schedule.
