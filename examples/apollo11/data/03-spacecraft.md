# Spacecraft and Launch Vehicle

Apollo 11 relied on three major pieces of hardware: the Saturn V rocket that launched the stack from Earth, the Command and Service Module Columbia that carried the crew to and from lunar orbit, and the Lunar Module Eagle that carried two astronauts down to the surface and back.

## Saturn V Launch Vehicle

The Saturn V was a three-stage, liquid-fueled rocket, the largest and most powerful launch vehicle flown to that point. Fully stacked with the Apollo spacecraft on top, it stood about 363 feet (111 meters) tall. Its first stage (S-IC) burned kerosene (RP-1) and liquid oxygen through five F-1 engines, producing roughly 7.5 million pounds of thrust at liftoff. The second stage (S-II) and third stage (S-IVB) burned liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen through J-2 engines. The S-IVB third stage placed the Apollo spacecraft into a parking orbit around Earth and later reignited to perform Trans-Lunar Injection, the burn that sent the crew toward the Moon. The Saturn V was developed under the direction of rocket engineer Wernher von Braun and built by NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center along with contractors including Boeing, North American Aviation, and Douglas Aircraft.

## Command and Service Module: Columbia

The Command and Service Module (CSM), named Columbia, was the mothership that carried all three astronauts from Earth to lunar orbit and back. It consisted of two parts: the cone-shaped Command Module, which housed the crew cabin, flight controls, and heat shield for the return to Earth, and the cylindrical Service Module attached behind it, which held the main propulsion engine, fuel cells for electrical power, and oxygen and water supplies. Columbia was built by North American Aviation. Only the Command Module returned to Earth; the Service Module was jettisoned and burned up in the atmosphere shortly before reentry. Columbia is the spacecraft in which Michael Collins remained while Armstrong and Aldrin were on the lunar surface, described in `02-crew.md`.

## Lunar Module: Eagle

The Lunar Module (LM), named Eagle, was the two-stage vehicle designed exclusively to operate in the vacuum of space and on the lunar surface; it was never intended to fly through an atmosphere and had no aerodynamic shape or heat shield. It was built by Grumman Aircraft Engineering Corporation. Its descent stage carried a throttleable engine, landing legs, and most of the consumables, and remained on the Moon permanently, serving as a launch platform for the ascent stage. The ascent stage housed the crew cabin and a separate engine used to lift Armstrong and Aldrin back off the surface to rendezvous with Columbia. Eagle had no seats; the astronauts stood, restrained by cables, during descent and ascent.

## Docking and Undocking

Columbia and Eagle were connected nose-to-nose through a docking tunnel that allowed the astronauts to crawl between the two vehicles without a spacewalk. On July 20, 1969, Armstrong and Aldrin moved into Eagle, undocked from Columbia, and separately fired Eagle's descent engine to begin the descent to the surface, while Collins remained aboard Columbia in lunar orbit. The precise timing of undocking and landing is covered in `04-timeline.md`.
