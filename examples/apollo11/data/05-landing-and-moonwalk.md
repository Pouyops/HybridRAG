# The Landing and the Moonwalk

## The Final Approach

As Eagle descended toward the Sea of Tranquility on July 20, 1969, its guidance computer triggered a series of "1202" and "1201" program alarms. Mission Control's guidance officer, Steve Bales, quickly determined the alarms indicated the computer was overloaded with rendezvous-radar data it did not need during descent, not a hardware failure, and gave the "go" to continue. At the same time, Armstrong saw that the computer's automatic targeting was steering Eagle toward a boulder-strewn crater. He took semi-manual control, pitching the Lunar Module forward to fly beyond the hazard and searching for a clearer patch of ground while fuel ran low. Eagle landed with roughly 25 to 30 seconds of descent propellant remaining, a far narrower margin than planners had intended. The landing itself and its timing relative to undocking from Columbia are detailed in `04-timeline.md`.

## "The Eagle Has Landed"

Immediately after touchdown, Armstrong radioed Mission Control in Houston: "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed." The call sign "Tranquility Base" named the landing site itself, on the southwestern edge of the Sea of Tranquility (Mare Tranquillitatis), a large basaltic plain on the Moon's near side chosen for its relatively flat, obstacle-free terrain.

## First Steps

Several hours after landing, Armstrong and Aldrin depressurized Eagle's cabin and opened the hatch. Armstrong descended the ladder first and, upon placing his boot on the lunar surface, spoke the words: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." The phrase was broadcast live to a worldwide television audience estimated at several hundred million people, one of the largest audiences for a single event in history at that time. Aldrin followed him down the ladder about 19 minutes later, becoming the second person to walk on the Moon, and described the view with the phrase "magnificent desolation."

## Activities on the Surface

Armstrong and Aldrin spent roughly two and a half hours working outside Eagle. Their tasks included:

- Collecting a contingency sample of lunar soil early in the excursion, in case the moonwalk had to be cut short.
- Planting a U.S. flag fitted with a horizontal rod so it would appear to fly in the airless environment.
- Setting up the Early Apollo Scientific Experiments Package (EASEP), which included a passive seismometer to detect moonquakes and a Laser Ranging Retroreflector, a mirror array used from Earth to measure the Earth-Moon distance with high precision; scientists continue to use it for laser ranging experiments today.
- Speaking by radio with President Richard Nixon, who called the astronauts from the Oval Office during the moonwalk.
- Collecting rock and soil samples, ultimately gathering about 21.5 kilograms (47.5 pounds) of lunar material to bring back to Earth for study.
- Leaving a commemorative plaque on Eagle's descent stage reading, in part, "We came in peace for all mankind," along with mission patches and medals honoring fallen American and Soviet astronauts and cosmonauts.

Armstrong and Aldrin then climbed back into Eagle, repressurized the cabin, and rested before the ascent described in `04-timeline.md`.
